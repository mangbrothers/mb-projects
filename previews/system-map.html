Plain-language guide to every part

For a reader who is educated but not technical: what each part of the system is, and what each of its pieces does.

What this is

Mangiapane Brothers runs its business on a private web application it built for itself, reached at apps.mangbrothers.com. Owners, office staff, crew managers, crew members and door-hanger distributors all sign in at the same front door with their Google account. Each person then sees only the pages their role needs.

Behind those pages sit a handful of services, each with one job:

  • a database that holds the records;
  • an automated service that sends email, files documents and charges cards;
  • QuickBooks for the books and payments;
  • Google Workspace for company email and file storage;
  • a phone system for calls and texts.

Most small service companies run on paper, text threads and a dozen disconnected apps. This system puts every customer, job, worker, signature and payment in one place, with clear rules about who owns each kind of data.

How to read the map

The map draws each part of the system as a box ("card") and the flow of information as arrows. Two ideas explain most of it.

1. One owner per kind of data.

  • The CRM owns customer records.
  • QuickBooks owns money received and the books.
  • The Team Manager owns worker records.

Every other system holds a copy, never the master.

2. Sensitive data goes the shortest possible way.

  • A customer's card number goes from their browser straight to the payment processor (Intuit). The company never receives it.
  • A new hire's tax and bank forms are locked on his own phone before they are sent. Only the owner can unlock them.

The subprojects

1. Front door (the portal)

The single sign-in page for everyone who works for the company. A person signs in with Google once, and the system checks the company's team list to decide where he lands. Owners and office staff land on Projects, crew managers on their crew's route, crew members on their schedule, and door-hanger distributors on their routes. Someone not on the list is turned away with a plain explanation.

  • Google sign-in: no new passwords for staff to remember or lose. Google's security, including two-step verification, protects every account.
  • Team list and access levels: one list decides who may enter and at what level: admin, crew (manager or member), or walker (door hanger).
  • Return-to-page: someone who follows a link while signed out is sent back to that exact page after signing in.

2. Projects (the admin dashboard)

The owners' task manager: every company initiative, from the fall marketing order to securing a second crew truck, broken into tasks with owners, due dates and categories.

  • Projects and tasks: each initiative with its tasks, who is responsible and the deadline.
  • Timeline and deadlines: key dates grouped by week, color-coded by category (hiring, sales, ordering, equipment, service, digital infrastructure, admin and finance).
  • Team list: where admins add people and set their access level.
  • Passwords and links: one-tap links to the company's outside services (QuickBooks, Canva, the phone system) with the shared sign-in details, for admins only.
  • Files: documents attached to projects.

3. CRM (customer relationship management)

The system of record for every lead and customer: contact details, where each lead stands, every call and note, the services sold and their prices, signed contracts, and billing status.

  • Leads board: each lead is a card that moves across stages (New, Contacted, Quoted, Active) by drag and drop, on a computer, iPad or phone.
  • Clients: the full record for each customer, with services, prices, contracts, payment method on file (brand and last four digits only) and billing status.
  • Quote and agreement builder: builds the seasonal services agreement with live pricing by neighborhood and service, ready to sign in person or by email.
  • Charges to run: every charge on the schedule: due now, waiting for a job to be finished, failed, or charged. It also holds the one-tap "charge card on file" and refund buttons.
  • Service log: every visit a crew completes, which bills only after a manager approves the day.
  • Contracts: every signed agreement, locked so it can never be edited after signing.

4. Customer signing and payment

The page a customer opens from the quote email. They read the quote and the full agreement, sign by typing their name, and save a card. The card number goes straight to Intuit, the payment processor, which hands back a one-time token. The amount due at signing is charged on the spot and a receipt is emailed.

  • Emailed quote: a clear table of services, the total and what is charged today, with one button.
  • Signature: a consent box and a typed signature, stamped with the time, device and a digital fingerprint of the exact text signed.
  • Card step: the card goes to Intuit only. It is protected by Google reCAPTCHA against bots, closes after three declines, and has a hard cap per charge.
  • Signed copy: a PDF of the agreement and the cancellation notice is saved to company Drive and emailed to the customer.
  • $1 test agreement: a safe way to test the whole path end to end with $1 charges that are then refunded.

5. The signing service (automation engine)

An automated service that runs inside the company's Google account and does the jobs no person should have to remember. It runs on demand and on a schedule every five minutes.

  • Email: sends quotes, signed copies, receipts, refunds, paperwork links and reminders from the company address.
  • Documents: saves signed agreements, employee reports and personnel files as PDFs in organized Drive folders.
  • Payments: connects to QuickBooks Payments to save cards, charge, refund and void. It has built-in limits, and a charge can never run twice.
  • Billing: charges approved work automatically, such as snow visits past the season limit, light installs and landscaping balances, with a receipt each time.
  • Hiring: imports job applications from Indeed emails onto the hiring board and numbers employee reports.
  • Paperwork and training: sends new-hire paperwork links, stores sealed forms, and files training material in Drive.

6. Crews (routes and schedules)

The page crews work from on their phones. The office sees every crew; a manager sees his crew; a member sees himself.

  • Day and week boards (office): every crew's routes and progress on one screen.
  • Routes and map: numbered stops on a street map, with directions, check-offs, photos and notes. Snow routes start when a storm is called.
  • Clock in and out: time records for every shift.
  • Crew log and day approval (manager): the manager reviews the day, fixes missed clock-outs and approves it. Approval unlocks billing and approves the hours.
  • Schedule: each person's next month of shifts, storm call-outs and meetings.
  • Hours and pay log: past hours by pay period and what payroll paid.
  • Training tab: training for his role, and any paperwork still to sign.
  • See as: the office can look at the page exactly as any crew member sees it.

7. Team Manager (people operations)

The office's workforce system: hiring, personnel files, crew assignments, paperwork, training, employee reports and payroll inputs.

  • Onboarding board: applicants move by drag and drop through Applied, Phone screen, Interview, Offer, Hired, Backup and Not hired. Indeed applications arrive automatically. Moving someone to Hired asks first, then creates his employee profile and sends his paperwork.
  • Personnel: each employee's profile, contact and emergency information, files, shifts, pay log and a running personnel file of calls, interviews, notes and reports.
  • Crews: who is on each crew and who manages it.
  • Paperwork: every person grouped by position and legal status (W-2 employee or 1099 contractor), with each form's status and an Email button per form.
  • Training: the library of slide decks, how-to procedures (SOPs), videos and documents, assigned by role or by person, with progress.
  • Reports: every employee report by reference number, filed by crew managers from their daily log.
  • Payroll: pay rates by job and role, pay periods and what was paid.
  • Past: former employees, with their whole record archived and restorable.

8. Employee paperwork and the vault

A new hire signs everything online from his phone: his offer, the company's privacy policy, and the official federal and Michigan tax forms (W-4, MI-W4), the I-9 and direct deposit. Contractors sign the W-9 instead. The page fills in the real government PDFs and places his drawn signature on them.

  • Packet by position: the system knows which forms each role and legal status needs.
  • Offer first: signing the offer is what opens his crew pages. Until then he sees only his paperwork and training.
  • The vault: tax, I-9 and bank forms are encrypted on his phone before they are sent. Opening one takes a separate paperwork password, and only the owner and admins he names can try it. Everyone in the office can see that a form is signed; almost no one can read it.
  • I-9 Section 2: the office checks his original documents in person and completes the employer section, with a paper I-9 kept as a backup.
  • Privacy policy: the written policy Michigan law requires for Social Security numbers, extended to all worker information. Every worker signs that he received it.

9. Routes (door-hanger distribution)

The page for door-hanger distributors, the company's main way of finding new customers. Each walker sees his assigned routes on a map, checks off streets with a photo as proof, and logs his daily count. The office sees every route, walker and count.

10. Previews

An admin page showing every customer-facing and employee-facing screen with sample data, built from the live pages. It is used to review changes with the admin team and to demonstrate the system to outside audiences without exposing real customers or employees.

11. Public website

mangbrothers.com, the marketing site customers find. It is hosted separately from the internal system and carries the company's privacy policy and terms of service.

The supporting services (cards on the map)

  • Firebase Realtime Database: the system's database, where records live and update live on every screen. A set of access rules, tested automatically, decides exactly who may read or change each kind of record.
  • Google Workspace: company email (admin@mangbrothers.com) and Drive, where signed agreements, personnel folders, paperwork and training files are stored.
  • QuickBooks: the books, invoices, payments (through QuickBooks Payments, approved by Intuit) and payroll. QuickBooks is the system of record for money.
  • Intuit Payments: the card processor. Card numbers go to Intuit only.
  • Quo: the company's phone system for calls, texts, voicemail and transcripts.
  • GitHub: where the application's code lives and is published to the web. Every change is recorded and can be rolled back.
  • Claude (AI assistant): builds and maintains the system, and carries data between systems that have no direct link yet (for example the phone system and the CRM). By design, it cannot open the employee paperwork vault on its own.

Why it matters

  • One source of truth. Every customer, worker, job and payment has exactly one home, so the numbers agree.
  • Approval before money moves. Nothing is billed until a manager approves the work, and no charge can run twice.
  • Built-in compliance. Locked contracts with a three-day cancellation window, the I-9 and tax forms done correctly, a written privacy policy, and records kept and destroyed on schedule.
  • Security by design. Card numbers never touch company systems, sensitive employee forms are encrypted end to end, and each person sees only what their role needs.
  • Built to scale. Adding a crew, a manager or a new hire is a few taps. The same system serves two crews or twenty.

Weak points in the plan, with a recommendation for each

Payroll authority is assigned to the wrong system

The site cannot be the system of record for payroll. It does not calculate withholding, file taxes or issue pay stubs.

Make the site the system of record for time records. QuickBooks Payroll stays the system of record for pay. An admin approves hours before they leave the site.

Claude is a single point of failure

Every transfer depends on one personal account, one Mac and a Chrome window that must be open. Nothing runs unless someone asks. A missed session means a missed client record.

Give Claude its own limited sign-in to the database, which the access rules already allow for. Run the Quo and QuickBooks transfers as scheduled tasks. Keep a queue with receipts and a daily reconciliation count.

Money received has two entry points

An admin records a payment in the CRM at signing. QuickBooks records the same payment when the card clears or the deposit lands. The two will drift.

QuickBooks owns money received. The CRM entry at signing is a claim. Claude confirms it against QuickBooks and flags every mismatch.

Update October 15: once card mode is on, the card charge at signing is recorded in QuickBooks by the signing service itself, which closes this gap for card payments. Checks and bank payments still need the cross-check.

A password page on the site is a liability

A home-built page in Firebase has no encryption at rest that you control, no audit trail and no safe way to rotate access. One rules mistake exposes bank logins.

Buy a password manager with shared vaults by tier. Link to it from the portal. Client door codes that crews need belong on the job record, not in the vault.

Drive cannot replace Firebase

Drive stores files. The pages need a database. Moving "all data" to Drive would break every page.

Keep Firebase as the database. Move only documents to a company shared Drive: signed agreements, worker paperwork, templates.

The domain email decision is made

Resolved October 9. admin@mangbrothers.com moved from Microsoft 365 to Google Workspace as one shared user. The signing service moved to admin@ on October 14 and now sends through Google. Microsoft 365 is still paid for.

Cancel Microsoft 365 after the old-mailbox pull and two clean weeks of signing mail from admin@.

Card payments now depend on one QuickBooks connection

If the connection to QuickBooks lapses or is revoked, card charges at signing stop. The signing service refreshes it daily and emails admin@ at most every 12 hours when it needs a reconnect, but someone has to act on that email.

Keep admin@ a QuickBooks company admin. Treat a reconnect email as same-day work. Card charges that fail are held for a person, never retried blind.

admin@ carries the whole money path

The signing service, contract mail, the QuickBooks connection and the reCAPTCHA keys all live under admin@mangbrothers.com. The Google Workspace trial ends October 21.

Set up Workspace billing before October 21. Keep 2-step verification on every account that can sign in to admin@ or the CRM.

The public site should not be the door

Staff login links on a marketing site invite attack and clutter the page customers see.

Put the portal on its own address, such as portal.mangbrothers.com, with no link from the public site. Build the client portal last. QuickBooks invoices already carry a pay link.

Decided October 9: how Claude connects, now and later

Now. The Google connection on Russel's Claude account points at admin@mangbrothers.com. The business is primary on this account. Personal and school tasks are secondary and get revised separately. No personal or school mail is forwarded into admin@, because that login is shared by the admin team.

At the move to a Team plan. Split into two Claude accounts: a company account connected to admin@ Google, Quo and QuickBooks, and a personal account connected to Russel's own Gmail for school and personal tasks.

Laptop out of the loop. Claude gets its own limited sign-in to the database so scheduled tasks run with the Mac closed. Updates arrive hourly at best.

Long-term option, not planned. For real-time updates: Quo notifies a small hosted program when a call ends, that program calls Claude through the API and writes to the CRM. It needs paid hosting and upkeep, so it waits until hourly is too slow.

Scope for the next month is still too wide

The workforce side is a second product: routes, maps, verification, profiles, documents and pay status. Starting it before the client loop runs unattended splits attention during the busiest sales window.

Next month is the client loop only: Quo to CRM, CRM to QuickBooks, payment status back. Start the workforce pages after that loop has run on a schedule for two clean weeks.
