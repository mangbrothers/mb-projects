## What this handbook covers
- The conduct rules for every crew member and crew manager.
- The attendance policy: personal days, sick time and unexcused absences.
- The crew conduct quiz tests this handbook. Read every slide.

---slide---
## Conduct rules in one place
- Uniform worn as issued. Layers underneath are fine.
- No swearing. No smoking, vaping or tobacco except on lunch, away from client property and the truck.
- No phones during a job. No calls except true emergencies.
- Music through headphones or earbuds only, never a speaker.
- Report damage immediately. Never hide it.

---slide---
## The workday
- Your manager sets the meet time and place. It is a neutral spot, not a client's house.
- The day starts with a short brief and a safety talk. Be there on time and ready.
- The crew follows the house order set in the system.
- Lunch is called by the manager and can run up to an hour. The crew stays together.
- The company supplies snacks.
note: Draft timing is meet 7:45, a 10-minute brief, first house by 8:00. Start times are not final.

---slide---
## Two seasons
- Attendance counts by season.
- Lawn season is spring and summer.
- Fall season covers fall cleanups, holiday lights and snow.
- Your count resets at the start of each season.

---slide---
## Personal days
- Personal days are unpaid.
- Request them through the system. See Using the site.
- A request made a week ahead is approved by default.
- Shorter notice is up to the operations manager.
- You get up to three approved personal days per season.

---slide---
## Vacations
- Vacations belong in the off-season.
- Personal days are for occasional time off, not a week away during the busy season.

---slide---
## Sick time
- The company follows the Michigan earned sick time law.
- Earned sick time is the only paid time off.
- Sick time is separate from your three personal days.
- New hires can use earned sick time after 120 days on the job.

---slide---
## Calling in sick
- Appointments and planned sick days: request them 7 days ahead in the system.
- Sick the night before: call the operations manager that night.
- Sick that morning: call the operations manager by 6:30 a.m.
- A text alone does not count. Call.
- Calling after 6:30 a.m. counts as one unexcused absence, unless a true emergency prevented the call.

---slide---
## Unexcused absences
- Skipping a day after a request was denied counts as one unexcused absence.
- Calling in sick after 6:30 a.m. counts as one unexcused absence.
- No request or call, and no show, counts as two unexcused absences.
- Three unexcused absences in a season means termination.

---slide---
## Snow is different
- Snow work happens when it snows, not on a fixed schedule.
- When a snow alert goes out, reply "confirmed" within 30 minutes.
- No reply means a call from the operations manager.
- The full snow alert rules are in the Snow module.

---slide---
## Firing offenses
- Three unexcused absences in a season.
- Hiding damage on a property, or gross negligence on the job.
- Working under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or running equipment unsafely.
- Taking work, cash or side jobs from a client without the owner's approval.
- Running equipment before 7 a.m. or after 11 p.m.
note: Matches section 7 of the employment agreements. The snow firing offenses (route order, salting before clearing) are in the Snow module.

---slide---
## Questions
- Ask your crew manager first.
- Attendance, scheduling and time off go to the operations manager.
