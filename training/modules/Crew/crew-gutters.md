## How we clean gutters
- Ladder up onto the roof. {reveal}
- Blow the gutters out from the roof. {reveal}
- Most of our houses are ranches, so most roofs are walkable. {reveal}
![Crew member blowing gutters from a ranch roof](image:gutters-from-roof)

---slide---
## Ladder safety
- Set the ladder on firm, level ground. {reveal}
- The ladder extends three feet above the roof edge. {reveal}
- The ladder is held or spotted while anyone climbs. {reveal}
- Three points of contact while climbing: two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand. {reveal}
![Ladder held at the base](image:ladder-spotted)

---slide---
## Roof rules
- No one on a wet, frosty or icy roof. {reveal}
- Your manager makes the call when a roof is too slippery to go on. {reveal}
- You may refuse a roof you think is unsafe, with no penalty. {reveal}
- Stay back from the edge except to work the gutter. {reveal}

---slide---
## Standard gutters
- Blow standard leaves out of the gutter into the yard. {reveal}
- Those leaves get cleared with the rest of the fall cleanup. {reveal}
- Check the downspout top is clear before you move on. {reveal}

---slide---
## Overgrown gutters
- Plants growing in the gutter or packed dirt cannot be blown out. {reveal}
- Those gutters are scooped out by hand. {reveal}
- Tell your manager when you find one, so it is recorded and billed. {reveal}
![Overgrown gutter being scooped by hand](image:gutters-hand-scoop)

---slide---
## Two-story and steep roofs
- Two-story homes and roofs too steep to walk mean the ladder has to be moved along the house. {reveal}
- Tell your manager before you start, so the extra work is recorded. {reveal}
- Never stand on the top rungs to reach farther. Move the ladder. {reveal}

---slide---
## Before you come down
- Every run of gutter is clear. {reveal}
- Nothing left on the roof: tools, debris or the blower. {reveal}
- Your spotter is at the ladder before you climb down. {reveal}
